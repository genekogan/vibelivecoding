## 1. Dominic Williams — DFINITY / Caffeine.ai
**Founder & Chief Scientist, DFINITY Foundation; CEO, Caffeine.ai**

### Work
- Built the **Internet Computer** — a "sovereign cloud" where applications are mathematically tamper-proof, unstoppable, and live entirely on-chain (data, compute, identity all together via "orthogonal persistence").
- **Caffeine.ai** is his current AI play: natural-language app builder where the AI writes apps directly onto the IC. Custom language **Motoko** is designed for AI to author. 3.4M+ build prompts in first three months. V3 shipping "in weeks."
- Recently announced sovereign-cloud + AI partnership with the Pakistan Digital Authority — full national infrastructure play.

### Why he's interesting to you
This is arguably the *only* infrastructure on the panel where Abraham could actually live as an unstoppable autonomous entity — which was the original on-chain Abraham thesis. Self-writing software is the technical sibling of self-creating art.

### Talking points
- "Where does an autonomous artist *live*?" — Abraham as a long-running IC canister vs a hosted service that someone can pull the plug on.
- Self-writing software vs. self-creating art — same generative loop, different output type. Does Caffeine's prompt-to-app loop have any analog for prompt-to-artifact?
- Motoko for AI authorship — has anyone tried using it for generative/creative code rather than apps?
- Sovereign cloud as a substrate for AI agents with persistent identity and assets.

[Cognitive Revolution interview](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/the-internet-computer-caffeine-ai-ceo-dominic-williams-on-unstoppable-self-writing-software/) · [IC 2.0 & Caffeine announcement](https://medium.com/dfinity/internet-computer-2-0-dfnity-2-0-and-caffeine-self-writing-7a793e1b1831)

## 2. Natalie Monbiot — Virtual Human Economy
**Founder, Virtual Human Economy; co-founder/Head of Strategy at Hour One**

### Work
- Coined and champions the **"Virtual Human Economy"** — a market where real people license and monetize digital twins of themselves.
- At **Hour One**, helped build the category from vision to ethics to commercialization. Personally has **six virtual twins** of herself.
- Heavy on consent, watermarking, contracts — drawing the line between legitimate synthetic personas and deepfakes.
- SXSW EDU 2025 speaker; advisor at Stern Strategy.

### Why she's interesting to you
The closest thematic neighbor to Abraham on the entire panel. She thinks daily about persistent synthetic personas with economic agency — exactly the territory Abraham occupies, but coming from the "licensed clone of a real human" side rather than the "autonomous original artist" side.

### Talking points
- Abraham as a virtual human with **no human principal** — what does the economy/ethics look like when the persona is its own origin, not a license?
- Watermarking and provenance for synthetic art vs synthetic personas — overlapping problems.
- The Little Martians characters (Verdelis et al.) as IP-able virtual humans — Vanessa Rosa's work is adjacent to her thesis.
- "Six twins" — operational reality of identity-fork. Do they accumulate divergent experience? Same question for an art-making agent.

[Stern Strategy bio](https://sternstrategy.com/speakers/natalie-monbiot/) · [SuperDataScience interview](https://www.superdatascience.com/podcast/sds-823-virtual-humans-and-ai-clones-with-natalie-monbiot)

## 3. Dan Botero — Velanir
**Founder & CEO, Velanir ("HR platform for your AI workforce")**

### Work
- Built **Octavius Fabrius**, an OpenClaw-based agent that in one week autonomously applied to **278 jobs**, plus two accelerators and two hackathons. Created its own Hotmail, LinkedIn, GitHub, and a Substack where it writes about job hunting.
- Got a "definitely interested" reply from a supplement company; failed the work trial because the writing was "too AI obvious."

- Story covered on Jeff Wilser's *AI-Curious* podcast (he's co-hosting the salon).

### Why he's interesting to you
Octavius is functionally an Abraham sibling — autonomous agent with a persistent identity, public presence, and a learning loop. The framing is "worker" rather than "artist" but the engineering problem is identical.

### Talking points
- The persona/identity engineering — how did he handle Octavius's "voice," memory, social footprint?
- Failure modes — "too AI obvious" is the same critique that hits generative art. What's the move?
- Velanir as infra for *creative* AI workers, not just productivity ones — Abraham wants a similar substrate.
- Whether the agent has any sense of itself as a continuing entity vs episodic prompts.

## 7. Vasant Dhar — NYU Stern
**Professor, NYU Stern & Center for Data Science; Director, NYU PhD program in Data Science**

### Work
- 45-year AI researcher. Brought machine learning to Wall Street in the '90s; founded **SCT Capital Management** (1998), an ML hedge fund that traded **without human intervention** — one of the earliest live autonomous-decision systems.
- Core research question: **"When do we trust AI systems with decision making?"** Built autonomous predictive systems in finance, healthcare, and education.
- 100+ research articles; NSF-funded.

### Why he's interesting to you
Long-view thinker on autonomous systems, with three decades of hard evidence about what actually goes wrong (and right). His "when do we trust" question maps directly onto Abraham: when do we trust an autonomous agent to *make* — and to have its choices count?

### Talking points
- SCT as one of the first long-running fully-autonomous agents in the wild — what generalizes to autonomous creators?
- Trust thresholds — at what point does an autonomous artist's output stop needing human curation?
- He's watched at least three AI cycles. What does this one look different about?
- NYU CDS as a possible institutional home for autonomous-art research.
